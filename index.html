Stage 2

The Build Round

Choose exactly one project from the bucket list below and build it beautifully, functionally, and independently. This is where the shortlist becomes the team.

01

Choose Your Build

Pick exactly one project from the five tracks: Analog Electronics, Digital Electronics, IoT & Robotics, Hardware & Software Orchestration, or Cybersecurity. Choose carefully. This is the project you will take from idea to prototype.

02

Build It Properly

Turn the idea into a working prototype. Complete the requirements, make the demonstration actually work, and pay attention to the details. We want to see something built, tested, and working.

03

Tell the Story

Document what you built using the provided Markdown template. Explain your approach, decisions, implementation, results, and what you learned. No AI-generated filler. Show your actual work.

Read Before You Build

Rules of Engagement

⚠
Build It. Own It. Prove It.

Your submission must represent your own work and your actual results. Fabricated demonstrations, falsified results, plagiarism, or deliberate misrepresentation will result in immediate disqualification.

  1. Choose once. Pick exactly one project and commit to it. Project changes require prior written approval from the panel.
  2. Build the whole thing. Every requirement listed under your project's Demonstration section must be implemented and demonstrated. If you claim it works, be ready to prove it.
  3. Show your work. Your documentation must follow the provided Markdown template and clearly explain what you built, how it works, what you tested, and what the results actually were. ↓ Get the template
  4. Give credit where it's due. Tutorials, libraries, references, and external material are allowed where appropriate, but copied work must be understood and properly credited. Passing someone else's work off as your own is a violation.
  5. Ship on time. You have approximately three weeks to complete the project. The submission deadline is fixed. Late submissions will not be evaluated unless the panel has been informed and approved an exception beforehand.
  6. Publish the evidence. Your public GitHub repository must contain the final code, documentation, and supporting evidence such as photographs, screenshots, waveforms, test results, or demonstration clips.
  7. Raise blockers early. Hardware failed? Stuck on a bug? Something isn't going to plan? Communicate it early. Honest progress and clear problem-solving matter far more than pretending everything worked.

Stage 2 · Final Submission

Make the work speak for itself.

Your build is ready when the code works, the documentation tells the story, and the demonstration proves it. Push the complete project to a public GitHub repository, then submit the repository link through the channel shared with you.
